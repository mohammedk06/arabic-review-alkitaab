أشعر بالخجل أحياناً
I Sometimes Feel Shy
◆ WHAT THIS LESSON LOCKS IN
جملة الصفة — the indefinite-noun relative clause with no relative pronoun and a required linking pronoun (this is the single highest-value structure in the lesson, and the one you'll trip on when reading). Plus أوزان الفعل as a system: the two new patterns VI تَفاعَل and VII اِنفَعَل, and the form↔meaning pairings (I→II causative, II→V reflexive, I→VII passive, III→VI reciprocal). Colloquially: قال / يقول لـ with a fused pronoun.

💡 ROOT PAYOFF
This lesson is where roots stop being trivia and become the wazn system. ع-ر-ف now spans three awzaan you own: عَرَفَ (I, to know) → عَرَّفَ (II, to introduce) → تَعَرَّفَ على (V, to get acquainted with) — plus المَعرِفة and التَعريف. Same move on ق-ط-ع: قَطَعَ (I, to cut) → اِنقَطَعَ (VII, to be cut off). And ج-م-ع ties تَجَمَّعَ (to gather) to جامِع (mosque, L10), جامِعة (university) and جَميع — one root, one idea of "gathering."
📎 Notes on usage (from the lesson)
1. بَينَ repeats with pronouns. Like English "between," it marks a location, a relationship, or a difference between two parties. If one or both parties is a pronoun, the word is repeated: كانت بَيني وبَينَه علاقة طيبة ثم انقطعت · شركتنا بَينَه وبَينَ الشارع الخامس.
2. خُطِبَت لـ is feminine-only, reflecting the traditional passive role of the woman in arranging marriage. For the man the verb is خَطَبَ ("to ask for someone's hand"). This is your first preview of the passive in Arabic — note the ـُـِـ vowelling.
3. سوق is usually مؤنث in formal Arabic, but can be مذكر — probably influenced by spoken usage, where it's normally مذكر.
4. عِندَما / لَمّا are narrative, not interrogative. "When (X happened), (Y happened)" — two clauses that usually match in tense: عندما ذاكرت الدرس مرّةً ثانية، فهمته أكثر. But sentences about the future only mark the future in the result clause: عندما أعود الى البيت سآكل العشاء ثم سأنام.
5. Egypt has two forms for "to be cut off" — in Cairo اِتقَطَع، يِتقِطِع is the more common one.
6. مِن with numbers = part of a group: هو واحد مِن أصحابي · ثلاثة مِن المهندسين · عدد مِن طلاب الصف. But the nonspecific quantity words بعض، عِدّة، مُعظَم، كل take no مِن — they are the first term of an إضافة: مُعظَم المدارس, عِدّة مواضيع, بعض الفنادق, كل الجرائد.
2 · Grammarالقواعد
2a · قال، يقول لـ — telling someone something (spoken)
The verb قال، يقول pairs with the preposition لـ to mean "to tell someone (something)." The لـ marks the person you are saying it to, and most often carries a pronoun. In spoken Arabic it's pronounced as if it were part of the verb:
DialectPhraseMeaning
الشاميبدّي أقوللَك شيI want to tell you something
المصريعايزة أقوللك حاجةI want to tell you something
الفصحىقال لـي / قال لـهhe told me / he told him
You'll hear the same fused لـ on other verbs soon. Learn it by imitating the speakers rather than assembling it piece by piece. Also listen for it in the "حضرتك مين؟" scene.
2b · جملة الصفة — the indefinite relative clause ★
A sentence used to define an indefinite noun. Because the noun is new to the listener (a friend, a woman, a picture), it needs more information attached — and Arabic attaches a whole sentence directly, exactly the way it would attach an indefinite adjective (صفة). Hence the name.
⚠ No linking word. English forces "who / that"; Arabic uses nothing after an indefinite noun. الذي / التي (and spoken اللي) are for definite nouns only.
العربيةEnglish (note the "who")
صديقي حامد استاذ يدرّس الادب العربي في جامعة القاهرة.… a professor who teaches …
تعرّفنا على امرأة تعمل في قسم اللغات.… a woman who works …
هذه صورة عمرها أكثر من ١٠٠ سنة.… a picture whose age is …
خُطِبَت لمهندس يعمل في السعودية.… to an engineer who works … (خالد, this lesson)
The linking pronoun (العائد). The described noun sits outside the structure of جملة الصفة, so the clause needs a pronoun standing in its place inside. When the clause is a جملة فعلية, that pronoun is often hidden inside the verb (it's the subject) — which is why examples 1 and 2 look like they have nothing:
صديقي حامد استاذ يدرّس الادب العربي → a professor [who] (he) teaches
But if the described noun is the object of the verb, or the object of a preposition, the pronoun must be written out to fill the hole it left behind:
الجملة الأصليةجملة الصفةEnglish
استمتعت بمباراة.شاهدت مباراة استمتعت بها.a game I liked [it]
دخّنت سيجارة.هذه أول سيجارة دخّنتها.the first cigarette I smoked [it]
ذهبت الى سوق.هذا أكبر سوق ذهبت إليه!the biggest market I've been to [it]
📏 Reading clue: any اسم نكرة immediately followed by a verb is almost certainly a جملة صفة. Train the eye on that pattern — it's the fastest comprehension win in this lesson.
2c · أوزان الفعل — new patterns VI & VII, and how awzaan relate
Two new patterns arrive with this chapter's vocabulary:
Waznماضي · مضارع · مصدرمثال
VIتَفاعَلَ ، يَتَفاعَل ، التَّفاعُلتَبادَلَ ، يَتَبادَل ، التَّبادُل
VIIاِنفَعَلَ ، يَنفَعِل ، الانفِعالاِنقَطَعَ ، يَنقَطِع ، الانقِطاع
How to spot them: VI begins with ت and its short vowels in الماضي and المضارع are all fatḥa (like V) — but it also has an alif (like III). That double resemblance isn't accidental: VI shares meaning with both. VII is unique in having a نْ. Mnemonic from the book: the n of انفعل matches the n in "seven."
Form and meaning — the three principles
1. Awzaan have meanings that relate to each other, not to an abstract reference point (VII relates to I; VI relates to III).
2. Generally, the longer and more complex the wazn, the more abstract the meaning (X is quite abstract). Awzaan with a shadda reflect extension or intensification.
3. Each wazn relates to one or two others in form and meaning. II & V both have shadda; III & VI both contain alif.
The four pairings to know now
PairRelationshipمثال
I → IIII adds a causative: "to make/cause someone to do X"عَرَفَ (to know) → عَرَّفَ بـ (to introduce)
زِعِل (to get upset) → زَعَّل (to upset someone)
II → VII is transitive (done to another); V is reflexive (done to oneself)عَرَّفَ → تَعَرَّفَ على (get acquainted)
ذَكَّرَ بـ (remind s.o.) → تَذَكَّرَ (recall)
سَوَّقَ (to market) → تَسَوَّقَ (to go shopping)
I → VIIVII is the passive of I (where I's meaning allows a passive). Only a minority do — so VII is relatively rare.قَطَعَ (to cut) → اِنقَطَعَ (to be cut)
III → VIReciprocal — think of VI as the "each other" waznتَبادَلَ (to exchange with each other)
Denominal bonus: II and V can be built from a noun. From سوق (market): سَوَّقَ، يُسَوِّق، التَسويق (to market something) and تَسَوَّقَ، يَتَسَوَّق، التَسَوُّق (lit. to take oneself to market → to go shopping).
📏 The move to internalize: stop memorizing verbs as isolated words. Log the root + wazn, and half the vocabulary list becomes derivable. Al-Kitaab Part 2 leans on this heavily.
3 · Culture & readings
Story: طارق and خالد on shyness (الخجل), friendships, and relationships with women — how open they are, whether they'd call themselves إنسان عاطفي. خالد explains the end of his relationship: she خُطِبَت لمهندس يعمل في السعودية — the single line that carries both the new passive preview and the جملة الصفة.

Culture — كيف هي صداقاتك وعلاقاتك؟: video interviews with بعض الشباب المصريين about their friendships — compare their framing of friendship and family closeness against your own.

Reading — برنامج تركيا (pp. 240–241): a real tour-agency itinerary from النخبة للسياحة والسفر — 13 days / 12 nights, دمشق → مرسين → ألانيا → أنطاليا → باموكالة → بورصة → يالوا → استانبول → كبادوكيا. Authentic-text practice: skimming a day-by-day timetable for جولة، فندق، سوق، بحر، جبل. Genuinely useful register if you end up doing regional travel/work.

Listening & dialogue: مع العائلة والأصدقاء; then "شو بدّي اعمل؟" (شامي) / "اعمل ايه في المشكلة دي؟" (مصري) — asking for advice about a problem.
4 · Common pitfalls
1Inserting الذي / التي after an indefinite noun — جملة الصفة takes no linking word. امرأة تعمل ✓ · امرأة التي تعمل ✗.
2Dropping the linking pronoun (العائد) when the noun is the object — مباراة استمتعت بها, سيجارة دخّنتها, سوق ذهبت إليه. English lets you delete it; Arabic does not.
3Putting مِن after مُعظَم / عِدّة / بعض / كل — those are the first term of an إضافة: مُعظَم المدارس ✓, مُعظَم من المدارس ✗. Numbers and عدد do take مِن.
4Confusing wazn V with wazn VI — both start with تـ and are all-fatḥa. V has a shadda (تَجَمَّعَ، تَعَرَّفَ); VI has an alif (تَبادَلَ). Both appear in this very vocabulary list.
5Not repeating بَينَ with a pronoun — بَيني وبَينَه, not بَيني وه.
6Treating عِندَما / لَمّا as a question word — they're narrative "when." The interrogative is مَتى. And in future sentences, mark the future only in the result clause.
